First meetup etiquette: consent, boundaries and communication

September 27, 2026 · Exchange team

Whether you're new to the lifestyle or have been part of it for years, first meetups follow a few simple principles. They all come back to respect: for the people you're meeting, for your partner, and for yourself.

Before you meet

Good meetups start with good conversations. Take time to chat before you meet in person. Ask about what the other person, couple or throuple is looking for, and share what you're looking for too.

  • Be honest in your profile and messages. Use recent photos and describe your experience accurately.
  • Talk about expectations. Is this a drink and conversation, or something more? It's fine if the answer is "let's see how it goes," as long as everyone agrees.
  • Respect "no" and "not yet". If someone isn't interested, thank them and move on gracefully.

Consent is the foundation

Consent in the lifestyle should be clear, enthusiastic and ongoing. That means:

  • Ask, don't assume. What someone agreed to in a message isn't a promise for the evening.
  • Anyone can change their mind at any time, and that's respected without argument or pressure.
  • In a couple or throuple, every partner needs to be comfortable. If one partner isn't, the answer is no.

At the meetup

Choose a public place for a first meeting. It keeps things relaxed and gives everyone an easy way to leave if the chemistry isn't there.

Keep your phone away, give the conversation your attention, and include everyone. If you're a couple or throuple meeting a single person, make sure they don't feel like an afterthought. If you're a single meeting a couple or throuple, talk to every partner.

Keep drinking moderate. Clear heads make for clear communication, and alcohol and consent don't mix well.

Privacy matters

Discretion is one of the most important values in the community. Don't share anyone's photos, name or personal details with others, and don't take photos of people without their permission. What you learn about someone stays with you.

Saying goodbye

Whether the evening was a great time or just a nice chat, end it kindly. A quick message the next day thanking them for their time is a good habit. If you'd like to meet again, say so. If you wouldn't, a polite "it was great to meet you, but I don't think we're the right match" is always better than disappearing.

Treat people well

The lifestyle community is smaller than it looks, and reputation matters. People remember those who were respectful, honest and fun to be around. Be that person, and you'll find doors open easily.
