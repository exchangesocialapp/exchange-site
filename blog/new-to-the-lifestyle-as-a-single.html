New to the lifestyle as a single? A beginner's guide

October 13, 2026 · Exchange team

Plenty of people come to the lifestyle on their own. Maybe you're curious, maybe you've been part of it with a past partner, or maybe you simply like meeting open-minded people. Whatever brought you here, and whatever your orientation, there's a place for you. Singles are a big part of the community, and the ones who have the best experiences tend to share a few habits.

Know what you're looking for

Before you message anyone, get clear with yourself. Are you hoping to meet couples, other singles, throuples, or you're not sure yet? Are you looking for friendship, social events, something ongoing, or something more casual? There's no wrong answer. Being honest about it in your profile saves everyone time and attracts the people you actually want to meet.

Respect the agreements you're stepping into

When you connect with a couple or a throuple, you're meeting people who already have agreements with each other. Those boundaries come first. Ask what they're comfortable with rather than assuming, and never try to talk anyone past a line they've drawn. Make sure every partner is part of the conversation, not just the one who messaged you first. If only one person seems enthusiastic, that's worth noticing.

Build a profile people trust

A thoughtful profile goes a long way. Write a few honest lines about who you are and what you enjoy, keep your public photos clear and friendly, and save personal photos for a private album you share only when you're comfortable. On Exchange, Exchange+ and Unlimited members can verify their identity for a verified badge, and members you've talked with can vouch for you. Both help you stand out as a real, respectful person.

Take it slow

A good first message is specific and polite. Mention something from their profile and ask a question. Don't send a wall of text or anything explicit before you've been invited to. When you do meet, a drink or a social event in a public place is the easiest start. There's no obligation for anything else to happen, and many lasting friendships in the community begin exactly that way.

Labels are optional

You may hear terms like "unicorn" for some single women or "bull" for some single men. Some people embrace these labels and some don't. Use the words that feel right to you, and never assume someone else fits a label because of how they look or who they're with. Straight, gay, bi, pan, queer or still figuring it out, you get to describe yourself.

Stay safe and trust your instincts

Tell a friend where you're going, arrange your own transportation, and keep personal details like your last name and address to yourself until you know someone. If something feels off at any point, leave. You can block and report anyone from their profile or a chat, and our team reviews every report.

Be honest, be patient, and treat people the way you'd want to be treated. The community tends to give back exactly what you bring to it.
